2. The Question This Module Answers 
The deleveraging_risk module answers one question continuously: 
Forced selling leaves fingerprints before the price moves. This module detects those fingerprints  across five signal layers. Each layer corresponds to a distinct mechanism in the deleveraging  cascade. The layers are ordered by historical lead time: signals that fire earliest carry the  highest weight. 
3. Five Signal Layers 
3.1 Signal Layer 1 — Funding Rate Stress 
Lead time confirmed: 7–9 days before October 10, 2025 · Weight: 0.35 
In BTC and ETH perpetual futures, funding rates measure the cost of holding leveraged long  positions. When leverage is excessive, funding rates spike positive — longs pay shorts to  maintain positions. When forced unwinds begin, funding rates collapse or go sharply negative  as longs flee.

3.2 Signal Layer 2 — Open Interest Fragility 
Lead time confirmed: 5–7 days before October 10, 2025 · Weight: 0.30 
Open interest is the total number of outstanding derivative contracts. Prior to October 10, OI had  expanded to $220 billion — a historic extreme. The combination of price flat or rising while OI  reaches a structural peak is the classic pre-crash signature. Post-crash OI fell by more than $70  billion in days. 
Two sub-signals combine into a single OI score. Peak detection: OI is at or near a 30-day high  while funding rate is simultaneously elevated. Collapse detection: OI drops more than 8% in 24  hours while price is flat or rising (stealth unwinding). 
Data source: Binance Open Interest Historical API (fapi/v1/openInterestHist) · Already in  binance_feed.py · No new feed required

3.3 Signal Layer 3 — Options Skew / Dealer Gamma 
Lead time estimated: 2–4 days before event · Weight: 0.15 · Needs Polygon.io  validation 
When market makers sell put protection to institutional buyers hedging large equity positions,  dealers accumulate negative gamma — they must sell into falling markets and buy into rising  markets to delta-hedge. When dealers flip from positive to negative gamma, small price moves  get amplified rather than dampened. This transition is detectable before the price move. 
Phase 1 implementation uses the CBOE SKEW index as a proxy (available via Yahoo Finance,  already wired). Phase 2 upgrade to precise QQQ put/call skew at 25-delta via Polygon.io once  that feed is live for the PCSA build. Josh builds the receptor now — the upgrade is a one-line  data source change. 
Phase 1 source: ^SKEW via Yahoo Finance (yahoo_feed.py · already wired) · Phase 2:  Polygon.io 25-delta QQQ skew · Feed-agnostic receptor pattern applies 
3.4 Signal Layer 4 — Margin Debt Structural Fragility 
Lead time: Weeks to months (structural, not tactical) · Weight: 0.12 
FINRA publishes monthly margin debt data with approximately a 3-week lag. Not useful for  intraday monitoring. The rate of change over the prior 2–3 months is one of the most reliable  structural indicators of forced deleveraging risk: when margin debt has grown more than 20% in  3 months, the system is fragile. When it starts contracting after rapid growth, forced selling  follows. This signal scores the structural environment, not the tactical trigger.

3.5 Signal Layer 5 — BTC/QQQ Correlation Spike 
Lead time: Less than 24 hours · Weight: 0.08 · Confirmation signal only 
In normal markets, correlations between asset classes are moderate and stable. In the 24–48  hours before a forced deleveraging event, correlations across seemingly unrelated assets spike  toward 1.0 as the same leveraged participants sell everything simultaneously. BTC and QQQ  falling together is not a leading indicator — it is confirmation that unified forced selling is  underway. This signal carries the lowest weight because it fires last. 
Implementation note: 5-day rolling Pearson correlation between BTC daily return and QQQ daily  return. Pure arithmetic on price data. No additional feed required. 
Data source: BTC-USD and QQQ daily close via Yahoo Finance · Already wired in  yahoo_feed.py · Pure rolling correlation arithmetic · No new feed required 
4. Composite Score Formula 
Each signal layer produces a sub-score 0.0–1.0. The composite deleveraging_risk score is the  weighted sum of all five sub-scores. Weights are calibrated against the October 10, 2025 event  — earlier signals carry higher weights because they provide more actionable lead time.

Python implementation 
def score(self, market_data: dict) -> float: 
 """ 
 Returns float 0.0-1.0. Higher = more deleveraging risk. 
 Weights calibrated against Oct 10 2025 event. 
 """ 
 s1 = self._funding_rate_score(market_data) # weight 0.35 
 s2 = self._oi_fragility_score(market_data) # weight 0.30 
 s3 = self._skew_score(market_data) # weight 0.15 
 s4 = self._margin_debt_score(market_data) # weight 0.12 
 s5 = self._correlation_score(market_data) # weight 0.08 
 composite = ( 
 s1 * 0.35 + 
 s2 * 0.30 + 
 s3 * 0.15 + 
 s4 * 0.12 + 
 s5 * 0.08 
 ) 
 return max(0.0, min(1.0, composite))
