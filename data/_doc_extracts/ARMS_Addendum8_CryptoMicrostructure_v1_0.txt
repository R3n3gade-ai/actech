2. The Question This Module Answers 
The October 10, 2025 event was not simply a large sell. It was a liquidation cascade — a  feedback loop where each forced sale consumed liquidity, widened spreads, and triggered the  next wave of liquidations. The five signals in this module detect the structural conditions that  make such a cascade possible: thin order books, crowded positioning, dense liquidation  clusters, basis breakdown, and smart money withdrawal. Each condition is detectable days  before any price event occurs.

3. Five Signal Layers 
3.1 Signal Layer 1 — Spot/Futures Basis Compression 
Lead time: 3–7 days · Weight: 0.30 · Earliest structural warning 
The basis is the annualized premium of futures price over spot price. In healthy bull markets,  futures trade above spot at 15–25% annualized — institutions pay to hold leveraged exposure.  When the basis compresses sharply toward zero or goes negative despite spot price holding  high, it signals that sophisticated participants are quietly unwinding futures exposure while retail  price has not yet caught up. This divergence is the first fracture in the market structure. 
October 10 2025 context: Prior to the crash, the basis had been supporting a thick layer of basis  trades and carry structures. When that structure began to compress under the weight of  elevated open interest, it signaled that the carry trade was becoming untenable. The basis  compression was a leading indicator that the crowded trade was unwinding before the price  reflected it. 
Data source: Binance quarterly futures vs perpetual price spread · Already in binance_feed.py  · No new feed required · Computed as: (quarterly_futures_price / perp_price − 1) annualized 
3.2 Signal Layer 2 — Order Book Depth Deterioration 
Lead time: 1–3 days · Weight: 0.25 · Market maker retreat signal 
Order book depth is the total bid-side liquidity within a given price range of the current market  price. When market makers reduce quoted size or widen spreads without a visible price  catalyst, it means they are anticipating a move they cannot safely hedge. In early October 2025,  BTC order book depth at 0.5% from mid-price fell from approximately $15.5 million to under $10  million before the crash — a 35% deterioration in available liquidity with no corresponding price  decline. 
This signal is the market maker's equivalent of the canary in the coal mine. They see the  liquidation cluster data, the funding rate stress, and the basis compression before retail  participants do. Their withdrawal of liquidity is itself a signal that they expect a disorderly move.

3.3 Signal Layer 3 — Liquidation Cluster Density 
Lead time: 2–5 days · Weight: 0.20 · The loaded gun signal 
Liquidation clusters are zones on the BTC price ladder where a large concentration of leveraged  long positions will be automatically liquidated if price reaches that level. The ResearchGate  academic analysis published post-crash confirmed that a BTC liquidation leverage heatmap  from October 6–14, 2025 showed concentrated liquidation zones directly at the levels reached  during the October 10–11 cascade. Those clusters were visible for days before the event. 
When a dense cluster sits within 8–12% below current price — exactly where the October 10  crash reached — any move toward it becomes self-fulfilling. The liquidation engine executes the  selling automatically, with no human decision required. The cluster is the loaded gun. The  macro trigger is the finger that pulls it.

3.4 Signal Layer 4 — Long/Short Ratio Extremity 
Lead time: 3–7 days · Weight: 0.15 · Crowded positioning signal 
When the long/short ratio on perpetual futures reaches extreme levels — above 70% longs  across major exchanges — the market is dangerously one-sided. Any adverse move forces the  majority to exit through the same narrow door simultaneously. The ratio itself does not predict  the direction of the move. It predicts the severity when a move occurs. At the time of the  October 10 crash, 88% of the $19.1 billion in liquidated positions were long positions — the  market had been overwhelmingly one-directional for days. 
Data source: Binance (wired) + OKX REST API + Bybit REST API · OKX and Bybit are new  lightweight plugins · Josh adds as src/data_feeds/okx_feed.py and bybit_feed.py · Simple  GET requests returning JSON · Estimated build: 0.5 days total for both 
Computation: weighted average of long% across three exchanges by OI share. Binance ~45%,  OKX ~30%, Bybit ~25% of combined perpetual volume. 
3.5 Signal Layer 5 — Stablecoin Exchange Outflow 
Lead time: 3–5 days · Weight: 0.10 · Smart money withdrawal signal 
Before major crashes, sophisticated participants convert crypto holdings to stablecoins on-chain  and withdraw them from exchanges — reducing their exchange-held collateral before the storm.  Net stablecoin outflows from major exchanges while BTC price holds high is a classic pre-crash  signature that has appeared before every major crypto deleveraging event since 2021. It  
represents smart money reducing exchange exposure before the liquidation cascade they  anticipate. 
This signal carries the lowest weight because it is the least precise — stablecoin flows have  many causes beyond crash anticipation. However, when it fires in conjunction with the other  four signals, it adds meaningful confirmation that the withdrawal is defensive rather than routine.

4. Composite Score Formula 
Each signal layer produces a sub-score 0.0–1.0. Weights are calibrated by lead time — the  basis compression signal fires earliest and carries the highest weight. The long/short and  stablecoin signals provide confirmation rather than primary warning. 
Python implementation 
def score(self, market_data: dict) -> float: 
 """ 
 Returns float 0.0-1.0. Higher = more microstructure fragility.  Weights calibrated against Oct 10 2025 cascade anatomy. 
 """ 
 s1 = self._basis_compression_score(market_data) # weight 0.30  s2 = self._order_book_depth_score(market_data) # weight 0.25  s3 = self._liquidation_cluster_score(market_data) # weight 0.20  s4 = self._long_short_ratio_score(market_data) # weight 0.15  s5 = self._stablecoin_outflow_score(market_data) # weight 0.10

5. ARAS Integration — Dual-Module Alert 
The crypto_microstructure module feeds the ARAS composite independently from  deleveraging_risk. The advisory contribution table is identical in structure. The critical addition is  a dual-module alert that fires when both modules are simultaneously elevated. 
Dual-module alert logic 
When both deleveraging_risk.score() and crypto_microstructure.score() simultaneously exceed  0.60, ARAS receives a combined advisory contribution of up to +0.12 to the composite score.  This dual-elevation condition is the specific signature of October 10, 2025 — active institutional  selling pressure meeting structural plumbing fragility simultaneously. The dual alert fires a  dedicated flag in the daily monitor EDR section and is logged permanently with both module  scores and timestamps.
